# Daily Market Brief — 2026-07-20

**TL;DR:** Equities sold off broadly as 10Y yields climbed to 4.57% and chip stocks extended last week's losses, though a falling VIX and Iran cease-fire signals capped the damage.

---

## What Moved Markets

**Rates & macro:** The 10Y Treasury yield rose 2.5 bps to 4.566%, weighing on growth stocks. The dollar edged up 0.15% to 100.90. No single macro catalyst; Middle East tension monitoring was the stated driver from bond desks.

**Equities:** S&P 500 -1.01%, Nasdaq -1.40%, Dow -0.77%, Russell 2000 -0.42%. Tech and semis led the decline, extending what CNBC flagged as chips' worst week in over a year. VIX fell 3.6% to 18.1, suggesting the selloff is orderly rather than panicked.

**Energy:** WTI crude -1.03% to $81.64 after Iran signaled openness to U.S. talks "based on national interests," deflating the geopolitical risk premium that had pushed pump prices above $4. A Dynacom tanker was struck near Oman, keeping the situation live.

**Gold / Crypto:** Gold essentially flat at $4,013. BTC -0.31% at $64,493; ETH -0.24% at $1,867. Both assets shrugged off the equity weakness.

## Stories Worth Knowing

- **AMD vs. Nvidia escalates:** AMD's Helios is its first full rack-scale AI system, directly targeting Nvidia's NVL72. Microsoft signing on as launch customer is a meaningful commercial signal, not just a demo win.

- **Iran war: 5 months in, oil surprisingly contained:** Reuters analysis explains why crude hasn't spiked despite sustained US-Iran conflict — spare OPEC+ capacity and demand softness are absorbing supply risk. A new cease-fire proposal surfaced Monday; Iran's response kept prices in check.

- **Houthis naval blockade threat:** Yemen's Houthis announced intent to blockade Saudi Arabia's coast. Watch for tanker insurance spreads and Red Sea routing costs to respond.

- **Chip week ahead:** Tesla and Alphabet kick off Mag-7 earnings. Semiconductor sentiment is fragile — TSMC's Arizona acceleration comments were constructive but weren't enough to lift the sector Monday.

- **UK politics:** Andy Burnham is the new UK Prime Minister. Trump publicly criticized Britain as "poverty stricken," adding a diplomatic friction point to watch for sterling and UK asset flows.
