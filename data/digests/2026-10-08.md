# Daily Market Brief — 2026-10-08

**TL;DR:** Oil surges 3.8% on Iran tanker attacks and Gulf hurricane risk, dragging tech lower and pushing the 10Y yield to 5.22% — stocks broadly weaker with Nasdaq taking the worst of it.

---

## What Moved Markets

**Oil & Inflation fears** were the session's dominant force. WTI jumped $3.39 to $91.66 on fresh Iranian tanker attacks and a hurricane threatening Gulf Coast production. That rekindled inflation anxiety already elevated by tariff data: the NY Fed attributed all price increases on many everyday items directly to tariffs.

**Rates** remained punishing despite a modest pullback — the 10Y yield eased slightly to 5.22% after touching multiyear highs, with traders digesting the latest bond auction. The level itself continues to pressure equities, especially long-duration tech.

**Equities** split by factor. The Dow held near flat (-0.08%) while the Nasdaq dropped 1.49%, a clear rotation out of rate-sensitive growth. VIX jumped 5.2% to 15.86, signaling rising hedging demand but not yet outright fear.

**Crypto** sold off hard alongside risk assets. BTC fell 3.2% to $80,601; ETH dropped 6.2% to $2,414 — the larger ETH move suggests some platform/leverage-specific pressure beyond macro.

## Stories Worth Knowing

- **Microsoft & Adobe green-card suspension:** The White House removed both from the labor department's foreign-worker certification program — a concrete operational hit to their ability to sponsor skilled immigrant workers.
- **Iran tanker escalation:** Multiple tanker attacks driving oil; Trump publicly ruled out a U.S. military strike before the November midterms, capping the geopolitical risk premium somewhat.
- **Fed's Waller:** Signaled more rate hikes are needed but offered "flexibility" on pace — not a pivot, but a slight softening of tone worth watching ahead of the next meeting.
- **NY Fed tariff study:** Official finding that tariff pass-through accounts for 100% of price increases on a range of consumer goods strengthens the case that core inflation is policy-driven, not demand-driven.
- **Jobless claims:** Hovering near a 57-year low for the fourth straight week — labor market still tight, giving the Fed limited cover to pause.
