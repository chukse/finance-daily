## What Moved Markets

**Equities soft, divergence by cap size.** S&P 500 -0.19%, Dow -0.59%, Russell 2000 -0.67% — small caps and value names bore the brunt. Nasdaq nearly flat (-0.05%) as mega-cap tech held up. VIX fell 3.75% to 17.95 despite headlines, suggesting the geopolitical spike was priced intraday.

**Geopolitics dominated sentiment.** U.S. conducted strikes on Iran; Iranian forces subsequently targeted Bahrain and Kuwait. Houthis threatened Saudi Arabian shipping lanes. Mediators are pushing a 10-day ceasefire window. This drove:
- **WTI crude +1.0% to $84.07** — crack spreads also widening (gasoline rising faster than crude)
- **Gold +1.25% to $4,060.60** — China central bank buying cited as additional support
- **10Y yield +1.7 bps to 4.606%** — modest risk-premium creep

**Dollar flat** at 101.02, caught between geopolitical safe-haven demand and softer recent inflation data.

**GM beat and raised guidance.** Revenue grew for the first time in over a year; management cited "resilient" consumer and stable pricing. Stock bounced; GM's memory chip commentary (positive read-through on demand) helped lift Micron, Sandisk, and AMD intraday.

**BlackRock raising $12B in bonds** to finance Meta's Texas data center — underscoring the scale of AI infrastructure capital commitment ahead of Alphabet's earnings.
