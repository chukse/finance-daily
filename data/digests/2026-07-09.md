# Daily Market Brief — 2026-07-09

**TL;DR:** Small-caps led a broad but bifurcated session — chips rallied on Micron's $250B investment pledge while mega-cap tech sold off, gold hit $4,130, and AstraZeneca cratered 9%.

---

## What Moved Markets

**Risk-on rotation, not risk-on consensus.** The Russell 2000 (+0.87%) outpaced the S&P 500 (+0.35%) and Dow (+0.13%) by a wide margin. VIX fell to 16.5 (-2.37%), 10Y yields eased slightly to 4.563%, and the dollar slipped 0.14% — a mild tailwind for risk assets broadly.

**Chips bifurcated.** Micron, Sandisk, and Marvell surged after Micron committed $250B in U.S. investment through 2035, boosting the domestic semiconductor narrative. QQQ gained +1.39% even as NVDA fell 1.74% — the rally was narrow within the sector.

**Mega-cap tech dragged.** GOOGL -2.00%, MSFT -1.55%, NVDA -1.74%, AAPL -0.89%, AMZN -0.72% all declined despite the Nasdaq's headline gain. The index's rise was carried by chips and mid-tier names, not the Mag-7.

**AstraZeneca -9%** after its Wainua heart drug missed its primary endpoint in a Phase III trial — a rare clinical failure for the company and the session's sharpest single-stock drop.

**Gold at $4,131 (+1.46%)** continued its run as a parallel safe-haven trade alongside equities. WTI crude fell 1.12% to $72.70 as U.S.-Iran diplomacy talk eased supply-risk premiums.

**PepsiCo missed estimates** — U.S. consumers pulling back on discretionary spending, consistent with the K-shaped consumption data circulating today.

- **Micron $250B U.S. commitment** through 2035 is the largest announced domestic chip investment on record and likely to feature in future CHIPS Act and tariff policy discussions.
- **Meta entering AI coding** directly challenges Anthropic and OpenAI in the developer tooling market; Meta also announced a $9B AI data center in Canada.
- **June home sales disappointed** while prices hit an all-time high — inventory remains the binding constraint, not demand destruction yet.
- **U.S.-Iran tensions easing** (per headlines referencing peace prospects) contributed to the oil pullback; market apparently giving diplomatic signals some weight.
- **Schwab strategists** issued a note arguing the era of easy index gains is over — notable timing given the S&P is at 7,509 and Mag-7 valuations are described elsewhere as "cheapest in a decade."
