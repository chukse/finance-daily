# Daily Market Brief — 2026-08-18

**TL;DR:** A global bond rout drove the 30-year Treasury to a 19-year high of 5.33% as the US-Iran truce expired and Hormuz closure fears reignited, sending VIX up 10% while equity index data was unavailable.

---

## What Moved Markets

**Bonds — the dominant story**
The 30-year Treasury yield hit 5.33%, its highest since 2007, while the 10-year rose 1.19% to 4.696%. The selloff reflects converging pressures: renewed Middle East risk after Iran said the Strait of Hormuz stays shut until the US meets interim deal conditions, persistent inflation concerns, and mounting US fiscal anxiety ("as US debt mounts, investors demand higher returns"). The bond market is effectively daring the Fed to hike.

**Middle East / Hormuz**
The US-Iran truce expired with no deal. Iran is blocking Hormuz pending conditions; Saudi Arabia is partially resuming oil loadings from inside the strait. WTI softened only modestly to $84.07 (-0.51%), suggesting markets see the disruption as manageable — for now.

**Volatility / Risk**
VIX jumped 10.3% to 15.72. S&P 500 futures fell pre-market. The dollar was essentially flat (-0.01% to 99.66), an unusual divergence from the bond stress.

**Gold**
$4,448/oz (+0.69%), supported by geopolitical risk and a BofA fund manager survey flagging gold as undervalued.
