# Daily Market Brief — 2026-10-14

**TL;DR:** Hot September CPI (3.7% headline, core above consensus) revived Fed rate-hike bets, spiked the 10Y to 5.24%, and pressured risk assets broadly — with Hormuz shipping attacks and an Aramco strike adding an oil-driven geopolitical layer.

---

## What Moved Markets

**Inflation + rates (the primary driver)**
September CPI came in at +3.7% YoY with core above expectations, putting a November Fed hike back on the table. The 10Y yield rose 2.4 bps to 5.24% — a level that continued to crowd out growth stocks. The dollar gained 0.64% to 102.83. Gold dropped 1.69% to $4,107, pressured by real-rate moves.

**Equities: tech and small-caps led lower**
- S&P 500 –0.88%, Nasdaq –1.31%, Russell 2000 –1.62% (most rate-sensitive, hit hardest)
- Dow nearly flat (–0.05%), cushioned by energy and financials mix
- VIX +5.5% to 16.1 — not panic, but hedging is picking up

**Geopolitical risk: Hormuz + Aramco**
Two cargo ships sank near the Strait of Hormuz as Iranian attacks escalated. A Houthi strike hit a building at Saudi Aramco's headquarters. Iran said it's "counting the moments" to strike U.S. warships. WTI crude rose 0.92% to $95.01. The IEA flagged market tightness separately. At least one Wall Street bank is now formally telling clients to trim tech in favor of energy.

**Banks: BofA drags, Morgan Stanley saves**
- Bank of America fell ~8% after missing Q3 revenue estimates and absorbing a $1.2B regulatory charge — a double hit
- Morgan Stanley beat handily; equities trading revenue was $909M above expectations — a stark contrast within the same sector

## Stories Worth Knowing

**Fed back in play.** Reuters reports a November rate hike is now seen as a tight call after the CPI print. The bond market is pricing it; the 5.24% 10Y is the lived consequence.

**Hormuz escalation is real.** Two ships sunk, Aramco facilities struck, Iran making explicit threats against U.S. naval assets. The Strait handles ~20% of global oil. This is no longer background noise — it's a supply-risk premium building into crude.

**Netflix cutting 1,000 jobs (~6% of workforce).** No earnings context yet, but a meaningful structural signal from a company that only recently returned to growth mode.

**GlobalFoundries secured up to $21B** in funding to build two chip plants — a significant onshoring/supply-chain story largely overlooked amid the macro noise.

**BlackRock's Fink warned** that a data center construction moratorium would trigger a recession — framing AI infrastructure as macro-critical, not just a sector theme.

**Morgan Stanley vs. BofA** sets up an interesting earnings-season read: active trading environments reward flow-heavy banks; net interest income and fee-dependent models are getting squeezed by the rate and regulatory environment simultaneously.
