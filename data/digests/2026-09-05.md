# Daily Market Brief — 2026-09-05

**TL;DR:** A blockbuster jobs report (162K vs. ~54K expected) pushed yields to 4.78%, pressured large-cap tech, and sent gold down 1.4% — but small-caps held up as rate-sensitive growth names bore the brunt.

---

## What Moved Markets

**Jobs shock drove the session.** September's payrolls print of 162,000 — roughly 3× consensus — reset Fed-cut expectations sharply. The 10Y yield climbed 2.2 bps to 4.784%, its highest recent level, weighing on long-duration assets.

**Rate-sensitive divergence was stark.** Large-caps fell (S&P -0.38%, Dow -0.51%) while the Russell 2000 gained +0.25%, suggesting some rotation into value/cyclicals that benefit from a stronger economy even at higher rates.

**Gold took the hardest hit** — down 1.38% to $4,430 — as the strong data lifted the dollar (+0.16% to 99.16) and reduced safe-haven demand. WTI crude held firm at $91.48 (+0.20%), partly supported by a U.S. strike on an Iranian tanker near Kharg Island, a meaningful geopolitical escalation.

**VIX ticked up 1.47% to 14.53**, modest but directionally consistent with repricing of rate risk.

## Stories Worth Knowing

- **Iran strike:** A U.S. attack hit an Iranian tanker near Kharg Island — Iran's primary oil export terminal. No supply disruption reported yet, but this is the kind of event that can move crude quickly if it escalates.

- **Ukraine diplomacy:** Witkoff and Kushner arrived in Moscow; Putin ordered a 72-hour halt to strikes on Kyiv during the visit. Ceasefire signaling remains fragile but is market-relevant for European energy.

- **Anthropic IPO:** Reuters reports the launch is targeting mid-October. At scale, this will be a key read on private AI valuations.

- **S&P 500 additions:** Bloom Energy, Illumina, and Everpure are joining the index — watch for passive-buying flows into those names ahead of inclusion.

- **Food dividends cut:** Two unnamed large food companies slashed dividends; three others quietly froze growth — a sector-level stress signal worth monitoring in consumer staples.
